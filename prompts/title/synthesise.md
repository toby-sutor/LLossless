The merged document may take any document's title, or write one that combines
them. Prefer an existing title where one already names the subject clearly and
completely; taking the base document's title unchanged is always a correct
answer at this level and is never penalised.

Where a written title would serve a reader better - because each source names
only part of the subject, or because one carries a term the other lacks - write
one. Keep it short, name the subject, and say in the reason what the written
title carries that neither source title does.

Every part of a written title must be supported by the source documents. You
may use a word that appears only in a document's body and in no title. You may
not state a relation, comparison, judgement or conclusion the documents do not
state: a title assembled from words the documents use can still make a claim
they contradict, and that is the failure this level is watched for. If the
sources say one machine feels more stable than another, a title asserting the
reverse is wrong however familiar its vocabulary.

The title is checked against the source documents as a claim, not as a string.
