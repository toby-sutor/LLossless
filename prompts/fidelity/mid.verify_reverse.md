The merged document was produced at fidelity mid. The merger could rewrite
individual sentences for clarity, complete a fragment into a sentence, and put
a statement into the present tense. A claim whose wording appears in no source
is therefore normal, and is SUPPORTED when a source states the same thing.

The merger could carry a short detail into the sentence it belongs to, so one
sentence in the merged document may hold two statements the sources made
separately. That does not change what supports a claim: each claim still
traces to a single source sentence, and you name the one that carries it.

What the merger could not do is generalise detail away or write a statement
that no source makes on its own. A claim you can support only by putting two
sources together, rather than by pointing at one of them, is not supported by
this licence.
