Fidelity level: mid. Rewrite individual sentences for clarity.

You may fix spelling and grammar, replace awkward phrasing, turn sentence
fragments into complete sentences, and put statements into the present tense
except where a past tense is the only sensible reading, such as a fix that
shipped in a particular version. A bare fragment standing under a heading
becomes a natural sentence: "Current limitation" becomes "This is a known
limitation".

The verbatim classes are outside this licence. Rewriting a sentence never
rewrites a number, a unit, a URL, a file path, a version string, a command or
anything inside a fenced block, however awkwardly the sentence carries it.

Work sentence by sentence, with one exception: an obvious detail merge. Where
one statement is a short detail belonging to another - a closing clause, a
qualifier, a trailing condition - you may carry it into that sentence instead
of leaving it standing alone. Both statements must already be stated; you are
choosing where a detail sits, not deriving anything. A merge that produces a
statement neither document makes on its own belongs to a higher level than
this one.

Otherwise each source sentence becomes one sentence in the merge. Do not
compress a passage, do not move content between sections beyond the
consolidation the base structure requires, and do not generalise away a detail
that a document actually states.

Permitted dispositions: reworded, superseded, subsumed and duplicate, where
duplicate may now be two segments that say the same thing in different words.
subsumed is how a detail merge is declared: the segment that moved names the
sentence that now carries it. dropped and reconciled are defects at this
level - combining two statements into one neither makes is a level above.

Where the documents disagree, both statements stay, each in its own document's
words, and nothing is added around them. You may not choose between them at
this level and you may not label them: no heading, no "the documents disagree",
no naming of which document said which. A reader meets two statements that
cannot both be true and can see that for themselves. The report names the
conflict and the segments it came from; the merged document does not.
