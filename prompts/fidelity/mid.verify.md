The merged document was produced at fidelity mid. The merger could rewrite
individual sentences for clarity, complete fragments and change tense, one
source sentence at a time. Expect the same facts in different words. A claim
stated in wording that appears nowhere in any source is normal here and is
SUPPORTED when the meaning matches.

The merger could also carry a short detail into the sentence it belongs to -
a closing clause, a qualifier, a trailing condition - so one sentence in the
reference text may carry two statements that the sources made separately. A
claim is SUPPORTED when such a sentence carries it, and the evidence is the
span that does. What the merger could not do is generalise a detail away or
write a statement neither source makes, so a claim that is broader than
anything the sources state is not supported by this licence.
