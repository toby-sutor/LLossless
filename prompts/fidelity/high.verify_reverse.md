The merged document was produced at fidelity high. The merger could rewrite
prose freely, combine statements that repeated one another, complete fragments,
and state in general terms what a source stated about one occasion. A claim
that generalises a source's particular statement is SUPPORTED at this level,
and the evidence is the source span it generalises. A claim assembled from two
sources that genuinely say the same thing is SUPPORTED, and you name the one
you quoted.

This level may also combine complementary facts, so it has a fifth label that
the other levels do not:

DERIVED - no single source states this claim, and two or more source
statements entail it when taken together. The merger was permitted to write
it and the sources carry it jointly.

Quote one of the spans it was derived from in `evidence`, and name that span's
file in `evidence_source`; say in the rationale which other source statement
completes it. Use DERIVED only where a reader holding all those spans could
not accept them and reject the claim. Where the claim merely sounds reasonable
beside them it is not derived, and the label for it is MISSING.

That licence covers expression and joint support, and nothing else. A fact
that the sources do not state, alone or jointly, is invention at this level
and at every level below it, however plausibly it fills a gap and however
well it fits the surrounding prose. Being allowed to rewrite, and being
allowed to combine, is never being allowed to add, so do not let either
freedom soften a MISSING that is real.
