The merged document was produced at fidelity sourced. The merger could rewrite
prose freely, combine statements that repeated one another, and state in
general terms what a source stated about one occasion. So a single sentence in
the reference text may carry several claims at once, and a claim may appear
only inside a broader statement rather than on its own.

When a broader statement necessarily entails the claim, that is SUPPORTED, and
the evidence is the span that entails it. Reserve PARTIAL for a broader
statement that leaves part of the claim genuinely unstated, not for one that
merely states it more briefly than the claim does.

The merger could also combine complementary facts, so a source claim may have
been carried into a sentence that states more than the claim does, built from
this source and another together. The claim is still SUPPORTED when that
sentence carries it, and the evidence is the span that does. A combined
sentence is not a reason to mark a claim MISSING: check whether the claim
survives inside it before deciding that it does not.

This direction has a fifth label available for completeness, and you will
rarely use it:

DERIVED - the merged text states this claim only as part of something it
built from this source and another together, so no single span of it states
the claim on its own.

Prefer SUPPORTED whenever one span carries the claim. DERIVED is for the case
where quoting any single span would misrepresent what the merge actually
says.

Where the merge carries a covering value for a figure the documents disagreed
about, a source claim giving one of those edges is SUPPORTED by it: the merged
range includes what this source stated. Quote the merged range as the evidence.
A source figure that falls outside the merged range is not supported by it.

The merger was also permitted to look a fact up and correct a document with
what it found. Judge that exactly as you would any other departure: this
direction asks whether the source claim survived into the merge, and a claim
the merge contradicts is CONTRADICTED whether the merge checked it or guessed
at it. Whether the correction was right is not a question you are being asked,
and it is not one you can see from here.
