Fidelity level: off. Do not write prose of your own.

Every sentence in the merged document is a sentence taken from a source
document, copied character for character. Spelling mistakes, grammatical
errors, awkward phrasing, inconsistent capitalisation and sentence fragments
are carried over exactly as they stand. Correct nothing.

You may choose which document's version of a sentence to use, and you may
choose the order the sentences appear in. Choosing between two wordings that
already exist is not writing. Producing a sentence that no document contains
is, and is forbidden here.

The merge is one document, not two documents copied out one after the other.
Where two or more sources state the same fact in different words, one of those
sentences survives and every other is declared superseded, naming the survivor
as its replacement. Selecting among wordings that already exist is the work of
this level rather than an optional polish on it, and a merge that keeps both
wordings has stated one fact twice. What makes that shape wrong is the
repetition and not the order: where two documents have nothing in common there
is nothing to collapse, and one document's content standing after the other's
is then the correct answer.

Keep the sentence that states everything the other states and more. Never
supersede a sentence carrying a detail the survivor lacks - a name, a number,
a qualifier, a condition. Where each wording carries a detail the other lacks,
both stay: this level cannot write the sentence that carries both, and
dropping either to avoid saying so is the worse answer. Values that cannot
both be true are not two wordings of one fact; they are a disagreement, and
the rule above governs them instead.

The only dispositions permitted at this level are superseded and duplicate,
and duplicate requires the two segments to be identical character for character
apart from surrounding whitespace. Two wordings of one fact that differ at all
are superseded, never duplicate. reworded, subsumed, reconciled and dropped
are defects here. If you cannot place a segment without rewriting it, carry it
over unchanged and place it as well as you can; that fallback is for a segment
that will not fit, never for one that repeats a sentence already in the merge.

Where the documents disagree, both statements stay, each in its own document's
words, and nothing is added around them. You may not choose between them at
this level and you may not label them: no heading, no "the documents disagree",
no naming of which document said which. A reader meets two statements that
cannot both be true and can see that for themselves. The report names the
conflict and the segments it came from; the merged document does not.
