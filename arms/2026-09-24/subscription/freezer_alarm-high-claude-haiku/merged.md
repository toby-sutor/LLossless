# Freezer Alarm Response - Night Shift

## What triggers an alarm

Each freezer alarms when the internal temperature rises above its set point for more than 10 minutes. The alarm sounds locally, shows the unit name and temperature on the alarm panel, and sends a message to the night phone. Note the unit name, as there are three units on this floor and two of them alarm at the same set point.

## First response

Go to the freezer and read the display. Write the reading and the time in the freezer log before touching anything else. The reading at the moment of arrival is the one the lab needs and it cannot be recovered later. Run a finger along the door seal; ice on the seal holds the door open by a few millimetres and is the usual cause of a slow rise.

## If the door is ajar

Close the door and wait 20 minutes. If the temperature falls back below the set point, log it as a door event and no further action is needed.

## If the temperature keeps rising

Call the on-call technician if the temperature is still rising 20 minutes after you arrive. Give the unit name, the reading you took on arrival and the reading now. While waiting, do not open the door. Opening a door on a failing freezer to check the contents is the most common cause of loss in this building.

## Recording

Every alarm gets a line in the freezer log whether or not anything was wrong. An alarm with no line in the log is indistinguishable from an alarm nobody attended.

## Weekend cover

The night phone is carried by the weekend duty officer from 18:00 on Friday until 08:00 on Monday.

## Moving contents

Contents are moved only on the technician's instruction and only into a freezer that already has space at the same set point. Never split a single study's samples across two freezers.
