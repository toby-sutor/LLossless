# Freezer Alarm Response - Night Shift

## What triggers an alarm

Each freezer alarms when the internal temperature rises above its set point for
more than 10 minutes.
The alarm sounds locally and sends a message to the night
phone.
For weekend cover, the night phone is carried by the weekend duty officer from 18:00 on Friday until 08:00 on Monday.

## First response

Go to the freezer and read the display.
The alarm panel names the unit and shows the temperature that triggered it.
Note
the unit name; there are three units on this floor and two of them alarm at the
same set point.
Write the reading and the time in the
freezer log before touching anything else.
The reading at the moment of arrival
is the one the lab needs and it cannot be recovered later.
Every alarm gets a line in the freezer log whether or not anything was wrong.
An
alarm with no line in the log is indistinguishable from an alarm nobody attended.

## If the door is ajar

Run a finger along the seal.
Ice on the seal holds the door open by a few
millimetres and is the usual cause of a slow rise.
Close the door and wait 20 minutes.
If the temperature falls back below the set
point, log it as a door event and no further action is needed.

## If the temperature keeps rising

Call the on-call technician if the temperature is still rising 20 minutes after
you arrive.
Give the unit name, the reading you took on arrival and the reading
now.
While waiting, do not open the door.
Opening a
door on a failing freezer to check the contents is the most common cause of loss
in this building.

## Moving contents

Contents are moved only on the technician's instruction and only into a freezer
that already has space at the same set point.
Never split a single study's
samples across two freezers.
