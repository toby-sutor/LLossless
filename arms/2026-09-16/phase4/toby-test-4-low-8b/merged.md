A Short Guide to Christianity

Christianity is the world's largest religion, with over 2,000,000,000 followers, and it began in the first century with Jesus of Nazareth, a Jewish rabbi who was crucified in Jerusalem around AD 30–33, whose followers proclaimed that he arose from the dead.

Its central beliefs are monotheism, the Trinity (God as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit), the incarnation and divinity of Jesus, and salvation by God's grace through faith in Christ's death and resurrection.

Its sacred text is the Bible, made up of the Old and New Testaments, which Christians regard as the inspired and authoritative Word of God.

Core practices include baptism as a rite of initiation, the Eucharist (communion) commemorating Jesus' last supper with his disciples, prayer, and communal worship in a church.

Over the centuries the faith divided into three main branches — Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy (split in the East–West Schism of 1054), and Protestantism (emerging from the 16th-century Reformation) — and today it is growing fastest in Africa and Asia while declining in much of the West.
