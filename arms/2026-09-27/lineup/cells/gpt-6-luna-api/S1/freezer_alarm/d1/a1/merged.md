# Freezer Alarm Response - Night Shift

## What triggers an alarm

Each freezer alarms when the internal temperature rises above its set point for more than 10 minutes. The alarm sounds locally and sends a message to the night phone.

## First response

Go to the freezer and read the display. The alarm panel names the unit and shows the temperature that triggered the alarm. Note the unit name: there are three units on this floor, and two of them alarm at the same set point. Write the reading and time in the freezer log before touching anything else. The reading at arrival is the one the lab needs and cannot be recovered later.

Every alarm gets a line in the freezer log, whether or not anything was wrong. An alarm with no line in the log is indistinguishable from an alarm nobody attended. From 18:00 on Friday until 08:00 on Monday, the night phone is carried by the weekend duty officer.

## If the door is ajar

Close the door and wait 20 minutes. If the temperature falls back below the set point, log it as a door event; no further action is needed. Run a finger along the seal. Ice on the seal holds the door open by a few millimetres and is the usual cause of a slow rise.

## If the temperature keeps rising

If the temperature is still rising 20 minutes after you arrive, call the on-call technician. Give the technician the unit name, the reading taken on arrival, and the current reading. While waiting, do not open the door. Opening a door on a failing freezer to check the contents is the most common cause of loss in this building.

## Moving contents

Contents are moved only on the technician's instruction and only into a freezer that already has space at the same set point. Never split a single study's samples across two freezers.
