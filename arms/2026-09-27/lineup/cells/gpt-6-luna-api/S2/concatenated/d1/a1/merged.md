# The Bells of Carrow Hill

Six bells hang in the tower on Carrow Hill, and no bell has been added or taken away since the tower was raised.

My grandmother counted the bells with me when I was small, and we found six of them in the tower above the village.

All six were cast in 1782 by a founder who put his mark on the tenor alone.

The founder cut the year 1782 into the tenor, and that was the only bell he signed.

A full peal needs eight ringers, so the band could never attempt one while a single ringer was missing.

We could not attempt a full peal with fewer than eight ringers, and one absence was enough to call the whole thing off.

Once the ringing starts a full peal runs for two hours, and the band works through it without a break.

From the first stroke to the last a full peal took two hours, and nobody left the ringing chamber in between.

The bells are rung on the first Sunday of every month, and the village has always kept the hour by them.

We rang on the first Sunday of the month, every month, and people in the village knew the hour from the sound.
