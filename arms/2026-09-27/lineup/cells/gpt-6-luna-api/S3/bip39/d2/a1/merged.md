# BIP: 39

## Abstract
This BIP describes a mnemonic code or mnemonic sentence—a group of easy-to-remember letters—and its conversion into a seed that can later be used to generate deterministic wallets with BIP-0032 or similar methods. It has two parts: generating the mnemonic and deriving the seed from it. The BIP-39 seed is not an ASCII string.

## Motivation
A mnemonic code or sentence is easier for people to use than raw binary or hexadecimal representations of wallet satoshis. The sentence could be written on paper or spoken over the telephone.

This guide provides a way to transport computer-generated determinism through a human-readable transcription. It is not a way to process user-created sentences, also known as brainwallets, into a wallet seed.

## Generating the mnemonic
The mnemonic encodes entropy in a multiple of 32 bits. More entropy improves security but increases the sentence length. The initial entropy length is referred to as ENT, which ranges from 128 to 256 bits.

First, an initial entropy of ENT bits is generated. A checksum is generated by taking the first ENT / 32 bits of the SHA-256 hash of the entropy. The checksum is appended to the end of the initial entropy. The concatenated bits are split into groups of 11 bits, each encoding a number from 0-2047 that serves as an index into a wordlist. Finally, we convert these numbers into words and use the joined words as a mnemonic sentence.

The following table describes the relation between the initial entropy length (ENT), the checksum length (CS), and the length of the generated mnemonic sentence (MS) in words.

 ```
 CS = ENT / 32
 MS = (ENT + CS) / 11
 
 |  ENT  | CS | ENT+CS |  MS  |
 +-------+----+--------+------+
 |  128  |  4 |   132  |  12  |
 |  160  |  5 |   165  |  15  |
 |  192  |  6 |   198  |  18  |
 |  224  |  7 |   231  |  21  |
 |  256  |  8 |   264  |  24  |
 ```
