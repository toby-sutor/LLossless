# Voyager 2 at Uranus

After encounters with Jupiter and Saturn, Voyager 2 continues to Uranus, a journey of about 4,5 years from Saturn. Its encounter with Jupiter is optimized in part to keep future planetary flybys possible. The geometry of the Uranus encounter preserves the possibility of a future encounter with Neptune. Voyager 1 has only 6.4 days of close study during its flyby of Saturn.

Voyager 2 is the first human-made object to fly past Uranus. Its short-range observations take place during the Jan. 24, 1986 flyby, when signals take approximately 2,5 hours to reach Earth. Sunlight at Uranus is five-hundred times weaker than at Earth. Closest approach occurs at 17:59 UT Jan. 24, 1986, at a range of about 81,500 kilometers (50,640 miles).

During its flyby, Voyager 2 discovers 10 new moons, including Puck, Portia, Juliet, Cressida, Rosalind, Belinda, Desdemona, Cordelia, Ophelia, and Bianca; their names continue the tradition of drawing on Shakespeare. It also discovers two new rings in addition to the nine previously known rings and finds a magnetic field tilted at 59 degrees to the planet’s rotation axis and displaced from its center. The spacecraft finds atmospheric winds reaching 450 mph (724 km/h), but no evidence of a boiling lake of water beneath the cloud tops. Uranus’s rings vary greatly in thickness and transparency.

Voyager 2 returns photographs of Miranda, Oberon, Ariel, Umbriel, and Titania, five of Uranus’s major moons. At a range of 17,560 miles (28,260 kilometers), its flyby of Miranda brings it closer to that moon than to any other object during its travels. Images show a surface with a striking mixture of seemingly unrelated features. Uranus itself appears generally featureless.

News of the Uranus encounter is interrupted by the Challenger accident, which kills seven astronauts during the space shuttle launch Jan. 28, 1986.
