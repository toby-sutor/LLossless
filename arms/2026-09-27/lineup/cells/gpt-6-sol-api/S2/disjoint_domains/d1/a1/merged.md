# Fire door inspection at the Millgate depot

Every fire door at the Millgate depot is inspected on a fixed round, and the result goes into a paper defect log at the gatehouse.

## Rounds

The duty officer walks the north corridor every weekday from 7 am till 8 am, right after the shift handover.
A second round covers the workshop and the paint store from 13:05 till 16:20 hours on Wednesdays only. The site manager has asked for a third round for years, but the budget has never allowed it.

## Weather

Frost swells the timber frames on the older doors, so between November and February several of them will not latch unless pulled hard.
Once the frames dry out in spring, the same doors close cleanly again.

## Contractor visits

While the doors are the responsibility of the site, the certified inspection is carried out by an outside contractor who attends every March 1st to March 31st.
During that visit the contractor tags each door, photographs the closer, and leaves a signed certificate at the gatehouse for the auditors.

## Records

It became custom that whoever finds a defect writes it in the log and pins a yellow card to the door itself. The card stays there until the repair is signed off.
Staff have started betting on whether the card or the written record is cleared first.
In most cases it is a tie, because one officer clears both at the end of the week.

# The Tuesday lunchtime chess club

The chess club has met in the small canteen on Tuesday lunchtimes ever since it was started by two apprentices from the drawing office.

## Season

The club does not meet between mid July and early September, because most members take leave and the canteen is closed for cleaning.
Play resumes on the first Tuesday of term. Over the years the members have learned to leave the boards set up rather than pack them away.

## Ladder

While outsiders assume the club is only for people who already play well, the truth is a good deal duller. Instead of a rating system, the club runs a simple ladder that anyone may join at any point.
Members who win a game move up one place, and the person they beat moves down one place.

## The county match

In one wet autumn the club was invited to play a county side, and nobody had expected the invitation. In a rush to field a full team, one apprentice agreed to play on the top board at two days' notice.
It took him the whole weekend to prepare, and he studied openings he had never used. He also lost his notes on the train.
But he arrived on time. He shook hands, sat down and played the quietest game of his life.
When the game was adjourned and the arbiter counted the material, the apprentice was a pawn ahead and offered a draw, which the county side accepted.

## Afterwards

When the match was over, the county side gave the club enough spare clocks to run every board on the ladder at the same time.
He travelled home and told everyone in the drawing office about the game until they asked him to stop.
Eventually the club grew to thirty members, and from then on the canteen staff put out an extra table every Tuesday so that nobody had to stand.
