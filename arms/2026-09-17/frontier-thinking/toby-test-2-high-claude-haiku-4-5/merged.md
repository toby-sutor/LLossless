How to take care of backyard chickens

Keep at least three hens, since chickens are social and stressed when alone, and allow roughly 4 sq ft per bird inside the coop plus 10 sq ft each in the run.

Predator-proofing is the most important aspect for safety: use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron around the perimeter against diggers, and fit two-step latches or an automatic door that closes at dusk.

Feed a complete layer ration supplemented by whatever they forage, and keep clean water available at all times, emptying and scrubbing the waterers often enough to stop algae and mould.

Provide 4-6 inches of absorbent bedding such as pine shavings, stir it weekly, top it up as needed, and strip it out every 6-12 months. Offer one nesting box per three to four hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird to help them feel safe and comfortable.

Finally, check the flock daily so you learn what "normal" looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction to avoid spread of illness and parasites, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
