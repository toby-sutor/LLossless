How to take care of backyard chickens
Keep at least 3 birds, as chickens are social animals and get easily stressed when alone. Provide roughly 4 sq ft per chicken inside the coop plus around 10 sq ft each in the bigger run.

Predator-proofing is the most important aspect of safety: use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron around the perimeter against foxes and other diggers, and use two-step latches or an automatic door that closes at dusk.

Provide designated chicken feed containing important vitamins and minerals, supplemented by whatever the chickens forage. Keep clean water available 24/7, and empty and scrub waterers often and thoroughly to stop algae and mould.

Add 4-6 inches of absorbent bedding, such as pine shavings, stir it weekly, top it up as needed, and replace it weekly; strip it out every 6-12 months. Offer 1 nesting box per 3/4 hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird to help them feel safe, comfortable, and orderly.

Finally, check the flock daily so you understand what "normal" behavior looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction to avoid the spread of illness and parasites, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
