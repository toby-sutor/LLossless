A Short Guide to Christianity

Christianity is the world's largest religion, with over two billion adherents, and it began in the first century with Jesus of Nazareth, a Jewish preacher crucified in Jerusalem around AD 30-33, whose followers proclaimed that he rose from the dead.

Its central beliefs are monotheism, the Trinity (God as Father, Son (Jesus), and the Holy Spirit), the incarnation and divinity of Jesus, and salvation by God's grace through faith in Christ's death and resurrection.

Its sacred text is the Bible, made up of the Old (Hebrew) and New (Greek) Testaments, which Christians regard as the inspired and authoritative Word of God.

Core practices include baptism as a rite of initiation, the Eucharist (communion) commemorating Jesus' last supper with his disciples, prayer, and communal worship in a church.

Over the centuries the faith divided into three main branches - Catholicism, Eastern Orthodoxy (split in the East-West Schism of 1054), and Protestantism (emerging from the 16th-century Reformation and itself split into hundreds or thousands of different sects) - and today it is growing fastest in Africa and Asia while declining in much of the Western world.
