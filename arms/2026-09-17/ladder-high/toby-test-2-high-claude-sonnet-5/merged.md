How to take care of backyard chickens

Keep at least 3 birds, since chickens are social animals that become stressed when kept alone, and provide roughly 4 sq ft per bird inside the coop plus around 10 sq ft each in the larger run.

Predator-proofing is the single biggest failure point for flock safety: use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron around the perimeter against foxes and other diggers, and fit two-step latches or an automatic door that closes at dusk.

Feed a complete layer ration that contains important vitamins and minerals, supplemented by whatever the birds forage, and keep clean water available 24/7, emptying and scrubbing the waterers often enough to stop algae and mould.

Add 4-6 inches of absorbent bedding such as pine shavings or similar, stirring it weekly, topping it up as needed, and stripping it out every 6-12 months. Offer one nesting box per three to four hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird, which will help them feel safe and comfortable and keep things orderly.

Finally, check the flock daily so you understand what "normal" behavior looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction to avoid spreading illness and parasites, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
