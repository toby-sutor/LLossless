Basic Care of a Backyard Laying Flock
Keep at least three hens, since chickens are social and stressed when alone, and allow roughly 4 sq ft per bird inside the coop plus 10 sq ft each in the run.

Predator-proofing is the single biggest failure point: use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron around the perimeter against diggers, and fit two-step latches or an automatic door that closes at dusk.

Feed a complete layer ration supplemented by whatever they forage, and keep clean water available at all times, emptying and scrubbing the waterers often enough to stop algae and mould.

Provide 4-6 inches of absorbent bedding (pine shavings or similar), stir it weekly, top it up as needed, and strip it out every 6-12 months; one nesting box per three to four hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird will keep things orderly.

Finally, check the flock daily so you learn what "normal" looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
