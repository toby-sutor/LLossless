How to take care of backyard chickens

Keep at least three hens, since chickens are social animals and become easily stressed when alone, and allow roughly 4 sq ft per bird inside the coop plus around 10 sq ft each in the bigger run.

Predator-proofing is the single biggest failure point: use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron around the perimeter against foxes and other diggers, and fit two-step latches or an automatic door that closes at dusk.

Feed a complete layer ration, which supplies the vitamins and minerals hens need, supplemented by whatever they forage, and keep clean water available at all times, emptying and scrubbing the waterers often and thoroughly to stop algae and mould.

Provide 4-6 inches of absorbent bedding (pine shavings or similar), stir it weekly, top it up as needed, and strip it out every 6-12 months. One nesting box per three to four hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird keeps things orderly and helps the birds feel safe and comfortable.

Finally, check the flock daily so you learn what "normal" behaviour looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction to avoid spreading illness and parasites, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
