How to take basic care of backyard chickens

Keep at least 3 birds, as chickens are social animals and get easily stressed when alone. Provide roughly 4 sq ft per chicken inside the coop plus around 10 sq ft each in the bigger run.

Predator-proofing is the most important aspect for safety. Use ¼" galvanised hardware cloth rather than chicken wire, bury a 12-inch apron in to the ground around the perimeter against foxes and diggers, and use an automatic door that closes at dusk or fit two-step latches.

Provide the chickens with designated chicken feed that contains important vitamins and minerals, such as a complete layer ration supplemented by whatever they forage. Provide clean water available 24/7. Clean the waterers often and thoroughly to stop algae and mould.

Add some absorbent bedding like pine shavings (around 4-6 inches), and replace it weekly, top it up as needed, and strip it out every 6-12 months. Offer 1 nesting box per three to four hens and 8-12 inches of roosting bar per bird will help them feel safe and comfy and keep things orderly.

Finally, it is important to check the flock daily so you understand what "normal" behavior looks like for each hen, quarantine any new birds for 7-10 days before introduction to avoid spread of illness and parasites, and compost manure before it goes anywhere near the vegetable beds.
