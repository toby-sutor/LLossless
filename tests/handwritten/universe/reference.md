Deep Time, Abridged

Sharks have been swimming for 450 million years - longer than trees have grown. Saturn's rings, though, are the young ones: only a few hundred million years old, which barely counts as ancient at all.

Oxford University was already teaching students when the Aztecs founded Tenochtitlán. And Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the building of the Great Pyramid - the pyramids were old news to her. Sudan, incidentally, has more of them than Egypt does.

We're bad at time. A million seconds is twelve days. A billion is thirty-two years. Pluto - a world smaller than Russia - was discovered in 1930 and still hasn't finished one orbit. Harriet Tubman's life overlapped with both Thomas Jefferson's and Ronald Reagan's.

We're bad at ourselves, too. Your stomach acid could dissolve a razor blade. You are technically blind while you sleep. More bacterial cells live in you than human ones. Bananas are berries; strawberries aren't.

And we're bad at maps. Maine is the U.S. state nearest Africa. Minnesota has more shoreline than California, Florida, and Hawaii combined. Every planet in the solar system, lined up, would fit in the gap between Earth and the Moon. There are more plastic flamingos than real ones, and Japan has a vending machine for every twenty-odd people.

Every atom in your body except the hydrogen was forged inside a star that exploded. You are old news too.
