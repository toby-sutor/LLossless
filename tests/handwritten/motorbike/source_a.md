Motorbike vs. quad (ATV)

A motorbike has two wheels and must be balanced by the rider, who leans the machine into corners (initiated by counter-steering), while a quad has four wheels and stays upright on its own thanks to its wide stance. Steering differs mechanically too: the quad turns its front wheels via linkage with Ackermann geometry - like a car - so the bars are turned into the corner, and it typically uses A-arm suspension and low-pressure tires for rough ground. Purpose-wise, motorbikes are built for paved roads and high-speed handling, whereas ATVs are built for low-speed torque, traction and terrain. Legally, motorbikes are road vehicles needing registration, insurance and a motorcycle licence plus helmet, while quads are usually classed as off-highway vehicles and often require a car licence if road-registered at all. Practically: a quad feels more stable at walking pace but is far easier to flip in a fast turn, since you can't lean it to counter the forces.
