MEMO: Linux - What the Name Means

Linux refers to the combination of the GNU Projects userland apps - announced by Richard Stallman in 1983 and largely complete by the early 1990s except for a working kernel - and the Linux kernel, released by Linux Torvalds in 1991 and licensed under GPLv2 from version 0.12 in 1992.  The FSF advocates the compound name of GNU plus Linux, not only "Linux", precisely because the linux kernel alone is not an OS: C libraries, system utilities, shell interpreter, compilers, and boo loaders do most of the work a user actually experiences. In practical terms, Linux distributions put together those software pieces differently - SUSE and Red Hat ship RPM-package based distributions, Debian and its derivatives, such as Ubuntu, use dpkg - but GNU is used regardless of the package format, with Android for mobile and IoT devices as the most popular exception that runs a Linux kernel without most of the other GNU software. Henceforth, the argument for the naming is not about pedantry but provenance: the GPL's license copyleft terms are what keep the entire stack auditable and redistributable, which is the difference between software you can inspect yourself and software you have to trust the vendor. 25 years of distro drift didn't change that underlying concept - the components may rotate, but the licence guarantees do not.
