iPhone — Pros
- 6–7 years of OS and security updates, delivered simultaneously to all supported devices
- Strongest resale value (roughly 60–70% after two years)
- Security and privacy solid by default; strict App Store review
- Best-in-class video recording
- Tight, seamless integration across Mac, iPad, Watch
- Consistent hardware-software optimization
- Reliable, long-lived hardware; easy hand-me-downs
- Simple, predictable experience with minimal configuration

iPhone — Cons
- High entry price; no genuine budget model
- Closed ecosystem; heavy lock-in (iMessage, AirDrop, Apple Watch)
- Limited hardware variety — no stylus, no rugged options
- Slower charging (20–40W)
- Less customization freedom
- No microSD expansion
- Higher repair costs
- Sideloading and third-party app flexibility restricted

Android — Pros
- Huge hardware variety across price points, designs, and form factors
- Devices from ~$100 to $2,000+
- Deep customization and control over the OS
- Faster charging (65–120W on many flagships)
- MicroSD expansion on select models
- More flexible third-party apps, launchers, and parental controls
- Better zoom cameras (Samsung) and AI camera options (Pixel)
- Choice of AI assistant and services
- 7 years of updates now standard on Pixel 8+ and Galaxy S25 series

Android — Cons
- Update longevity varies widely by manufacturer and model
- Weaker resale value (roughly 35–50% after two years)
- Openness increases malware/sideloading risk
- Fragmentation and inconsistent software experiences
- Quality varies enormously between cheap and premium devices
- Security patches often staggered or delayed
- Privacy protections less consistent by default
