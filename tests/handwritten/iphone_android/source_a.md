iPhone — Pros
- 6–7 years of OS and security updates, delivered to all supported devices at once
- Strong resale value (~60–70% after two years)
- Tight integration with Mac, iPad, Watch, AirDrop, iMessage
- Stricter app review and sandboxing; lower malware exposure
- Best-in-class video recording (Log, Cinematic, 4K high frame rate)
- Consistent UI, minimal configuration needed
- Apple Intelligence runs mostly on-device
- Reliable first-party support and repair channels

iPhone — Cons
- High entry price, no true budget tier
- Ecosystem lock-in makes leaving costly
- Limited hardware variety: no stylus, no rugged models
- No microSD expansion
- Slower charging (roughly 20–40 W)
- Customization still restricted compared to Android
- Sideloading and alternative stores limited/region-gated
- Closed source — no way to audit the platform

Android — Pros
- Huge price range ($150–$2,000+) and hardware variety
- Deep customization: launchers, default apps, system-level tweaks
- Sideloading, F-Droid, alternative stores
- Open source base — custom ROMs like LineageOS or GrapheneOS, degoogling possible
- microSD and headphone jack on some models
- Fast charging (65–120 W), larger batteries
- Pixel 8+ and recent Galaxy now offer 7 years of updates
- Better third-party parental control and automation tooling
- Choice of AI assistant, not a single vendor's

Android — Cons
- Update quality varies wildly by OEM; mid-range and budget often 2–3 years
- OEM bloatware and skins, inconsistent UX across brands
- Larger attack surface; sideloading shifts risk to the user
- Weaker resale value (~30–45% after two years)
- Fragmentation complicates app compatibility and enterprise management
- Video recording still behind iPhone
- Stock builds often heavily tied to Google services
- Fragmented support/repair experience per manufacturer
