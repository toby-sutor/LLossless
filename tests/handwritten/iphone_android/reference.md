iPhone — Pros
- 6–7 years of OS and security updates, delivered simultaneously to all supported devices
- Strongest resale value (roughly 60–70% after two years)
- Tight, seamless integration with Mac, iPad, Watch, AirDrop, iMessage
- Security and privacy solid by default; strict App Store review and sandboxing; lower malware exposure
- Best-in-class video recording (Log, Cinematic, 4K high frame rate)
- Consistent UI and hardware-software optimization
- Simple, predictable experience with minimal configuration needed
- Apple Intelligence runs mostly on-device
- Reliable, long-lived hardware; easy hand-me-downs
- Reliable first-party support and repair channels

iPhone — Cons
- High entry price; no genuine budget tier
- Closed ecosystem; heavy lock-in (iMessage, AirDrop, Apple Watch) makes leaving costly
- Limited hardware variety: no stylus, no rugged models
- No microSD expansion
- Slower charging (roughly 20–40 W)
- Less customization freedom compared to Android
- Sideloading, alternative stores, and third-party app flexibility limited/region-gated
- Higher repair costs
- Closed source — no way to audit the platform

Android — Pros
- Huge price range (~$100–$2,000+) and hardware variety across price points, designs, and form factors
- Deep customization and control over the OS: launchers, default apps, system-level tweaks
- Sideloading, F-Droid, alternative stores
- Open source base — custom ROMs like LineageOS or GrapheneOS, degoogling possible
- microSD expansion and headphone jack on some models
- Fast charging (65–120 W on many flagships), larger batteries
- Pixel 8+ and Galaxy S25 series now offer 7 years of updates as standard
- More flexible third-party apps, launchers, parental control, and automation tooling
- Better zoom cameras (Samsung) and AI camera options (Pixel)
- Choice of AI assistant and services, not a single vendor's

Android — Cons
- Update quality and longevity vary widely by OEM and model; mid-range and budget often 2–3 years
- Security patches often staggered or delayed
- OEM bloatware and skins; inconsistent UX across brands
- Larger attack surface; openness and sideloading shift malware risk to the user
- Privacy protections less consistent by default
- Weaker resale value (roughly 30–50% after two years)
- Fragmentation complicates app compatibility and enterprise management, and leads to inconsistent software experiences
- Quality varies enormously between cheap and premium devices
- Video recording still behind iPhone
- Stock builds often heavily tied to Google services
- Fragmented support/repair experience per manufacturer
